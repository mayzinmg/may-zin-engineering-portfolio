# About Page Content

## Who I Am

I have worked as a software engineer for many years. Living and working in Singapore—one of Southeast Asia’s major business hubs—has given me an environment where I can continue learning and growing professionally.

Data has become a foundation of modern systems. Organisations collect and transform it to generate insights, support decisions and prepare trustworthy inputs for AI. At the same time, the AI ecosystem is maturing, with greater attention being paid to security, governance and access control.

Technology moves quickly, and I believe engineers should never stop learning. I am especially drawn to experimentation, research and unfamiliar problems rather than repetitive work. Curiosity and the desire to understand how systems work are what keep me learning.

## What I Work On

I build and maintain business software using C# and SQL, with an emphasis on reliable data flows, system integrations and maintainable solutions. My current direction extends into data engineering, where I explore Azure Data Factory, Synapse, Databricks, Spark and lakehouse architecture through hands-on projects.

Alongside this, I study agentic AI—not merely how agents call tools, but how permissions, human approval, tracing, testing and guardrails can make them trustworthy. I am most interested in the point where practical engineering and responsible experimentation meet.

## How I Build and Learn
Before AI assistants, we learnt primarily from books, expert lectures, documentation and other people’s code. When we became stuck, finding a solution could take considerable time. Sometimes, the answer was available only because another developer had encountered the same problem, found a way out and documented it. We often had to explore many possible solutions, which could become a barrier to making progress.

AI assistants have changed that. We can now receive possible answers almost instantly, shortening the feedback loop and making unfamiliar subjects easier to approach. However, we still need the determination and resilience not to follow every suggestion blindly. We must question assumptions, resolve doubts, inspect generated work and test it before making a decision.

To ask useful questions and judge the answers, we need underlying knowledge. Engineers are not becoming extinct; we are evolving into more capable, AI-augmented engineers.
